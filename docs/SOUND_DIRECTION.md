# Sound direction

Permanent record of how the game's sound effects are made and why. Read it before adding or changing a sound.

## Aesthetic decision (author, 2026-11-12)

Sounds are deliberately crude and lo-fi, in the character of 90s formant / speech synthesizers: rough, buzzy,
unpolished. The author called the chosen jump sound "awful" on audition and said that is exactly the point:
it fits the game's concept. Consequences:

- Roughness is the goal. Do not "improve" a sound toward realism: no denoising, no reverb, no polish, no
  sample libraries, no recorded voices.
- Every shipped sound is original and synthesized by `tools/audio/generate_sounds.py`. This is a requirement,
  not a preference: originality and zero licensing risk (project-original, like `intro.wav`).
- Sounds are generated, never sampled. Every sound effect is now synthesized: the effects inherited from the Flame/Rust
  versions were all replaced in #313 (jump first, then the rest in three batches, below). Only music remains from
  elsewhere (`main_menu.mp3`, #189).

## Redo of all sounds in the jump's voice style (#313, author's decision 2026-11-13)

The author found the new jump unlike the rest and decided that **every shipped effect** is redone in the jump's
voice-synth style (breathy onset, formant vowel, buzzy and dark), auditioned by ear in batches. The author listens; the
agent cannot, so each batch ends with a list of what to hear and where.

## The recipe (as in `tools/audio/generate_sounds.py`)

Format for every file: plain PCM, 16-bit, mono, 44.1 kHz (libGDX's WAV decoder rejects other WAV flavours).
Each file gets a 4 ms linear fade in and out against clicks, its DC offset removed, and is scaled to a named
peak. Level target: effects are matched to the existing effects, which means RMS about -16 to -17.5 dBFS for
a prominent effect like the jump, peak never above -2 dBFS for it (the test enforces at most -1 dBFS for all).
Quiet background sounds sit well below (see each recipe).

### Jump: a breathy, throaty effort "ha!" (`JUMP_PARAMS`, seed 1302)

- Source: sawtooth glottal pulse train, pitch falling 190 -> 155 Hz over the sound, 2.5 % random period
  jitter, plus 10 % irregular pulse amplitude; in the last third extra period jitter and amplitude roughness
  (vocal fry, roughness field 0.15). Softened by a 3.5 kHz low-pass.
- Formants: three parallel band-pass filters for an open /a/, centre / bandwidth / gain: F1 760 / 190 Hz / 1.0,
  F2 1250 / 260 Hz / 0.55, F3 2550 / 420 Hz / 0.2. The bandwidths are wide on purpose (buzzy, not clean).
- Aspiration onset: 30 ms of noise band-passed around 1500 Hz (bandwidth 2500 Hz, roughly 500-3000 Hz), ramping
  up over 10 ms (breath, never a click), tailing off over 12 ms, at 1.10 times the vowel peak; the vowel starts
  at 35 % of the aspiration length so the two overlap.
- Envelope: vowel attack 16 ms, exponential decay with tau 65 ms, then a 12 ms linear cutoff at the very end
  (quick, slightly abrupt, no ring). Total duration 0.15 s.
- Colour and level: 3 kHz low-pass (dark, from the throat), then tanh soft saturation (`HA_DRIVE` 3.0) which
  lowers the crest factor so the RMS reaches the target; final peak -8.0 dBFS gives RMS about -17.0 dBFS.

### The other sounds

- `dust_puff.wav` (seed 2203, batch 1 of #313, second version after the author's audition): 0.14 s muffled "pfff" of air:
  noise through two 12 dB/oct low-passes at 1.3 kHz plus 15 % of the jump's /a/-formant noise (almost no vowel), 25 ms
  soft attack, tau 45 ms; RMS target -29 dBFS, capped by the peak at -14 dBFS (measured RMS -31.0). The first version
  (seed 2202, a whispered "hah" through the /a/ formants, 0.11 s) was too vowel-like; the very first was 0.10 s noise at 1.4 kHz.
- `wall_rumble.wav` (seed 3302, batch 3 of #313, accepted by the author 2026-11-13): a **seamless 1.0 s loop**, played with `Sound.loop` for the
  whole move time of the wall and stopped with `Sound.stop(id)` (D64). A very low growling voice: 50 glottal pulses per loop
  (50 Hz vocal fry; intervals jittered 15 % and renormalised to exactly 1 s, amplitude jittered 25 %) through three wide dark
  formants (300 / 520 / 900 Hz, an /o/ closing towards /u/), plus a little circular low-passed breath noise (35 %), tanh
  drive 4.0 (lowers the crest factor), a circular 500 Hz low-pass and a 3 Hz amplitude swell (3 whole cycles). Every filter
  runs over three copies and keeps the middle one, and there is no fade, so the wrap is as smooth as any other step (the
  loop-seam test covers it). RMS -20.0 dBFS, peak -9.2 dBFS: lower than the effects (-16.5 .. -19.4) because it can play for
  seconds, and 99 % of its energy is below 600 Hz (the test requires 80 %). The start and the stop are abrupt; if that is
  audible, add a volume ramp. Before: noise low-passed at 180 Hz plus a 55 Hz sine with modulation, seed 3301, RMS -17.8.
- `bramble_rustle.wav` (seed 6401, #364, D89, auditioned by the author 2026-11-17, approved): the bramble's D7 warning, 0.36 s of **dry cane snaps over a
  whispered bed**: 18 snaps of 12 ms noise with a 4 ms swell, band-passed around 3.2 kHz (bandwidth 1.8 kHz, centre varied
  +-25 %), at random times, over the `ha` /a/ formant breath (35 %, 30 ms swell, 160 ms decay), then a 5.5 kHz low-pass.
  Brighter than `grit_fall` (it is a warning, not a background), still unvoiced. RMS -27.3 dBFS, peak -9.0 (the cap limits it).
- `bramble_lash.wav` (seed 6402, #364, D89, auditioned by the author 2026-11-17, approved): the arm shooting out, 0.16 s of **a rising "shk"**: noise through
  a band-pass whose centre sweeps 1.2 to 4.2 kHz (bandwidth 900 Hz), 12 ms swell, 50 ms decay, tanh drive 3.0 to lower the
  crest factor. RMS -21.0 dBFS, peak -9.1. Both are played only while the bramble's box overlaps the camera's view, so a
  clocked trap off screen never ticks and one on screen is always heard.
- `thorn_spring.wav` (seed 6601, #446, D75 principle 4, accepted by the author 2026-11-28, [#520](https://github.com/jlogicgames/edgard_in_kimeria/issues/520)): the thorns turn lethal, 0.22 s of **a dull "thk" under a dry
  rasp**: a 330 Hz band-passed noise thump (bandwidth 260 Hz, 35 ms decay, the mound breaking) plus noise band-passed around 1.9 kHz
  (bandwidth 1.5 kHz) chopped by an 85 Hz pulse (70 % depth, the rasp), 6 ms swell, 70 ms decay, tanh drive 2.5, 4.5 kHz low-pass.
  RMS -26.0 dBFS, peak -9.0 (the cap limits it). One sound for `ThornRoot` and `ThornTuft` (one family, D93), played once in the frame
  the trap turns lethal (`ThornTrap.onSpring`, `Level.thornSprung`); the root also throws three specks in its mound's colours (`8a5a30`, `a89c78`: the first try, dark soil, sat at zero lightness distance from the forest's `2c3f66` and was dropped). **Shared with
  `bramble_lash`? No:** the lash is a smooth rising "shk" of a living arm; the thorns are hard, dry and chopped, and a thorn root
  beside a bramble must tell which one acted. They stay one family by level and length (both environment cues, ~0.2 s).
- `grit_fall.wav` (seed 4402, batch 3, accepted by the author 2026-11-13): 0.5 s of **whispered patter**: 24 grains of 18 ms noise, each with an
  8 ms swell (breath, no click) and band-passed around 1.9 kHz (bandwidth 1.5 kHz, centre varied +-25 %), grain times drawn
  from an exponential (tau 180 ms) so the density decays, then a 3 kHz low-pass. Unvoiced on purpose, like `dust_puff`:
  grains are scattered, and giving each a pitch would sound like a musical rattle, not falling grit. It shares the voice
  noise path (breath band, soft onset, dark). RMS -30.1 dBFS, peak -12.0 dBFS (the peak cap limits it: sparse grains have a
  high crest factor); before: 3.2 kHz clicks, RMS -36.7.

### Explosion (#324, seed 3501 and 3502, accepted 2026-11-13)

A low, rough, short "BOOM / fwoomph" in the same family: `render_voice` with a very low fried voice (85 -> 38 Hz, gruff /uh/
closing to /u/, fry from 15 %, jitter 8 %, 600 Hz low-pass), plus a dark noise burst (two passes of a 500 Hz low-pass, 10 ms
swell, 60 ms decay) and a falling sine sub tail (60 -> 34 Hz, 140 ms decay, starting 20 ms later), summed, tanh drive 3.0,
then RMS-matched. 0.45 s, RMS -15.0 dBFS, peak -7.2 dBFS (centroid 165 Hz, 68 % of the energy under 400 Hz). **It is
deliberately 2 dB fuller than the jump (-17.0)**: it plays only for a bomb or an explosive trap, so it is rare and may be
big. `tools/test_generate_sounds.py` therefore keeps it out of the voice set's 3 dB spread (`EXPLOSION_RMS_DB`) and
requires it to stay within that spread of the jump. It is also covered by the every-effect-audible test. If it is too
loud, too dull or too long, change `EXPLOSION_RMS_DB`, `EXPLOSION_VOICE` or the burst and sub constants.

### Stone impact (#347, seeds 3601 and 3602, accepted 2026-11-16)

A short "thud-crack" for every falling stone landing: `render_voice` with a low gruff falling /uh/ (105 -> 55 Hz, 1.5 kHz
low-pass, 70 ms decay), a 1.4 kHz band-passed noise crack (10 ms swell, 18 ms decay) on the hit and five soft pebble ticks
in the 50-220 ms after it, summed, tanh drive 3.0, RMS-matched. 0.30 s, RMS -16.5 dBFS (the hero's hit), peak -6.5 dBFS,
centroid 385 Hz. It is in the voice set's RMS table, so it stays within 3 dB of the jump. The crack swell was lengthened from
3 ms to 10 ms because the shared soft-start test (no click in the first 2 ms) failed at 3 ms. Preview: `art/audio/previews/stone_impact.png`.

### Branch impact (#443, seeds 7801 and 7802, D155, accepted 2026-11-28)

The falling branch's landing, **unvoiced**: a rush of air and rustling leaves, "like a real branch falling". Noise through a
band-pass whose centre sinks 3.2 kHz -> 900 Hz (bandwidth 1.8 kHz, 30 ms swell, 75 ms decay, the "shhukh"), then 34 leaf
grains of 12 to 34 ms around 3.8 kHz (each swelling and fading, starting 20-360 ms in, dense at the landing and thinning to a
third of the level), a 6.5 kHz low-pass, tanh drive 3.5, RMS-matched. 0.42 s, RMS -21.0 dBFS (4.5 dB under `stone_impact`),
peak -10.6 dBFS, centroid 3.5 kHz; in the ambient table of the test. The first version (0.22 s, a voiced /o/ falling to /u/
with a twig crack, -18.0 dBFS) was rejected on audition: the voice read as an "ou"; no vowel and no thud here. Every other
file regenerated byte-identical. Preview: `art/audio/previews/branch_impact.png`.

### Trip-line deadfall (#363, seeds 3701 and 3801, D88)

Two sounds, the audible half of D75 for the deadfall. **`deadfall_creak`**, the warning, plays at the trip: a strained
rope-and-wood creak, `render_voice` with a very low voice in constant vocal fry (a creak is fry: roughness 1.0 from the
start, jitter 0.12), rising 72 -> 112 Hz from /e/ to a strained /a/, 50 ms attack, 2.2 kHz low-pass
(`DEADFALL_CREAK_VOICE`). 0.60 s, so it ends before the base 0.8 s warning releases the log and never masks the swing; it is
not stretched by the longer-warnings assist. RMS -18.5 dBFS, peak -7.7 dBFS, centroid 605 Hz. **`deadfall_knock`**, the
consequence, plays when the ropes hit the snag, with six bark chips falling from it (`EnvironmentFx.barkChips`): a short,
dull wooden "dok", a closed /o/ falling to /u/ (150 -> 95 Hz), 4 ms attack, 50 ms decay, 1.4 kHz low-pass
(`DEADFALL_KNOCK_VOICE`). 0.18 s, RMS -17.0 dBFS, peak -5.1 dBFS, centroid 372 Hz. Both are in the voice set's RMS table.
The swing itself has no sound (the moving log is the signal) and the trap has no idle sound (it is visible). Every other
file regenerated byte-identical. Previews: `art/audio/previews/deadfall_creak.png`, `deadfall_knock.png`. Auditioned by the
author 2026-11-17, with the trap: approved.

| `button_click` (menu click; hover at 0.35 volume) | soft, short, closed "hi" | 2601 | 0.11 s | 250 -> 280 Hz | /i/ to /e/ | -19.4 / -12.8 |

`button_click` is deliberately short (0.11 s) and not the loudest, so the quiet hover version does not stack or smear when
the pointer moves across buttons. At 0.35 volume it sits about 9 dB lower. The level spread of the whole set is now 2.9 dB
(limit 3.0, unchanged).

`collect` is the quietest on purpose: it plays on every coin. The level test
(`tools/test_generate_sounds.py`, `VOICE_RMS_DB`) pins each converted sound to its target (+-0.5 dB), the peak cap and a
spread of at most 3 dB across the set; a sound joins that table when its replacement is generated.

### The boss's special attacks (#625, seeds 7901-7906, D157)

The boss's own low voice, so its calls never read as the hero's. The roots reuse `root_creak` and `root_thrust` and the rocks
land with `stone_impact` (look-alikes share a sound, D93). All sit inside the voice set's spread.

- `boss_call` (0.60 s): a rough roar rising 82 -> 150 Hz from /u/ to a strained /a/: the rocks' wind-up.
- `boss_blow_charge` (0.90 s; `_long` 1.35 s): a growl climbing 58 -> 130 Hz, chopped by pulses that quicken from 9 to 26 Hz,
  under a hiss that grows to the end; exactly the blow's warning.
- `boss_blow` (0.45 s): driven noise falling 2600 -> 250 Hz over a low thud.

**Audition: not verified** ([#625](https://github.com/jlogicgames/edgard_in_kimeria/issues/625)). Where they play:
`forest-boss` in `forest-2`, stand far for the call, close for the blow. Constants: `BOSS_CALL_*`, `BOSS_CHARGE_*`,
`BOSS_BLOW_*` in `tools/audio/generate_sounds.py`; previews `art/audio/previews/boss_call.png`, `boss_blow_charge.png`,
`boss_blow_charge_long.png`, `boss_blow.png`.

### RedMob whip and boss sweeps (#447, seeds 7101-7104, D93)

The consequence of three strikes that were silent (hazard audit #388, principle 4): the sound is heard where the strike lands,
hit or miss, once per strike, at the start of its active frame, and never after the boss is abandoned. The wind-up side stays in
[#240](https://github.com/jlogicgames/edgard_in_kimeria/issues/240). All three sit at -18.0 dBFS RMS, inside the voice set.

- `whip_crack` (0.16 s): a 34 ms rising swish (noise swept 1.5 -> 4.5 kHz, the lash cutting the air), then the crack: a 1.5 ms
  attack of band-passed noise around 3.6 kHz, driven hard, with a 22 ms decay. Centroid about 4.3 kHz, the brightest sound in the set.
- `boss_sweep` (0.30 s): a heavy axe whoosh, noise through a wide band-pass sweeping 450 -> 1800 Hz, swelling over 85 ms, driven.
- `boss_sweep_counter` (0.40 s): the same whoosh lower and longer (300 -> 1200 Hz), then a dull /u/ thud (`render_voice`, 110 -> 62 Hz)
  at 0.17 s for the axe's weight. Centroid 723 Hz against the normal sweep's 1862 Hz.

**Audition** ([#520](https://github.com/jlogicgames/edgard_in_kimeria/issues/520)): the author accepted all three on 2026-11-28, so the counter keeps its own sound. Where they play:
`forest-1-mob` (the whip: stand in reach, jump away during the wind-up, the crack still sounds) and `forest-boss` in
`forest-2` (a normal sweep missing you; hit the boss mid-swing to bring on the counter, step out of it).

**Why two sweep sounds:** the counter is the rare, uninterruptible strike the player was warned about for 1.15 s and it
reaches a taller box; the extra thud lets the player hear which sweep it was without looking.
Constants: `WHIP_*`, `BOSS_SWEEP_*`, `BOSS_COUNTER_*` in `tools/audio/generate_sounds.py`. Previews:
`art/audio/previews/whip_crack.png`, `boss_sweep.png`, `boss_sweep_counter.png`.

### `keepalive.wav`: not a sound effect (seed 5501, #316)

A seamless 1.0 s loop of noise (circular low-pass at 4 kHz) at **-65 dBFS RMS** (peak about -52 dBFS, about 18 LSB RMS of
16-bit). `KimeriaGame.updateAudioKeepAlive` loops it on desktop for as long as sounds are on, so an output device that
goes idle after about a second of digital silence (Bluetooth headphones, some macOS outputs) does not need ~0.5 s to wake
and swallow the start of the next real sound. Why this level: digital silence (zeros) is what devices treat as idle, so it
must be real signal; -65 dBFS is below the hiss floor of ordinary speakers and headphones at normal volume, but still
well above the 16-bit quantisation step. If it is audible on the author's device, lower `KEEPALIVE_RMS_DB`; if the
device still sleeps, raise it. **Not verified on hardware:** nobody has listened on macOS with the affected output (#316,
open). It is never played by gameplay code and does not count as a sound effect in tests.

## The Sound and Music switches ([D141](DECISIONS.md#d141-options-has-a-sound-switch-and-a-music-switch-494-2026-11-25), #494)

Options has two independent switches, both on by default and saved in the player's preferences.

| Switch | Silences | Where it is read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sound** | every `.wav` effect played as a libGDX `Sound`: one-shots, loops (`wall_rumble`), the menu hover and click, the desktop keep-alive loop | `KimeriaGame.playSounds()` and the play methods behind it |
| **Music** | the streamed `Music` tracks: `main_menu.mp3` and the story track `intro.wav` | `updateMenuMusic`, `updateIntroMusic` |

Switching applies at once: Sound off also ends the running loops, Music off stops the track with no fade. A one-shot already
playing (the click of the press that switched Sound off) ends by itself. A new effect goes through `KimeriaGame.playSound`
or `loopSound`; a new music track reads the Music switch.

## Making a new sound

1. In `tools/audio/generate_sounds.py` add a function with all parameters as named constants (with a comment)
   and a fixed seed for any randomness. Use only the standard library.
2. Add it to the `GAME_SOUNDS` table of outputs.
3. Regenerate: `python3 tools/audio/generate_sounds.py`. Do not touch the constants or seeds of existing
   sounds: the other files must stay byte-identical (compare md5 before and after).
4. Add the new file to the bounds table in `tools/test_generate_sounds.py` and run
   `python3 -m unittest discover -s tools -p 'test_*.py'`. Run
   `python3 tools/audio/analyze_sounds.py [--png art/audio/previews] FILE...`: it prints duration, peak and RMS
   (dBFS), DC offset, spectral centroid and the share of energy at or below 3 kHz and in 300-900 Hz, and
   writes a waveform-envelope PNG. These numbers check format, level, clicks and rough spectral shape. They
   cannot judge how the sound sounds.
5. Keep the file plain PCM 16-bit mono 44.1 kHz.
5b. Add the name to `Assets.SOUND_EFFECTS`: every effect is created at start-up, because a sound created on its first play
   was lost on the web (see `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`); `SoundPreloadTest` fails when a played name is missing.
6. Play it through `KimeriaGame.playSound("<name>")` (loads `assets/audio/<name>.wav`) behind the `playSounds()`
   check, like the existing callers. Add the name to `tools/check_web_release_artifact.py` only if its list
   of required files starts to cover audio effects (today it lists only `main_menu.mp3`).
7. Add the new preview PNG to `art/audio/previews/` and update `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`, "Original synthesized
   sound effects", if the origin or wiring status needs a note.

## Audition process

A human has to listen: objective numbers cannot judge the sound. What worked: produce a default plus ONE
alternative, never more than two, put the alternate outside `assets/` for the author, and let the author
choose. Delete the loser afterwards.

Design history (author's verdicts):

- Jump, first version, a "hup": it sounded like a spoken "PA!" (plosive); rejected.
- Jump, closed-mouth nasal "hm": too dull; rejected.
- Jump, throaty "ha!" with a breath onset: the breathy variant was chosen on 2026-11-12.
- `dust_puff`, `wall_rumble` and `grit_fall` were accepted at first listen. The rumble was then changed from a
  one-shot to a loop (2026-11-13) and needs a new listen. `dust_puff` also plays on landing at 0.6 volume.

## Don't

- No plosive onset: no click and no instant attack (it reads as "P"). Ramp in over at least about 10 ms.
- No clean modern vocal or polished sample-like sound; stay crude and buzzy.
- No long tails or ringing: effects end quickly; the end gets a short cutoff, not a reverb-like decay.
- No noise or tonal content that clips: peak at most -1 dBFS everywhere, -2 dBFS for the jump.
